🔁 Identity Habit Formation

Neurobiology & Identity Construction

The Architecture of Self-Transformation. Why grafting the habits of a disciplined future self onto an undisciplined present self guarantees relapse—behavior decoupled from identity is processed by the brain as a temporary deviation, not an intrinsic trait. Reverse-engineers the full change sequence: collapse the neural distance to the future self (vmPFC, age-progression, vivid visualization), let Bem's self-perception theory and Oyserman's identity-based motivation turn behavior into belief, offload control from the fragile prefrontal cortex to the basal-ganglia habit loop, pre-load If-Then implementation intentions and choice architecture, and reframe lapses as statistical outliers rather than identity regressions.

⚡ Neuroplasticity & Procrastination

Cognitive Neuroscience & Habit Kinetics

The Neuroscience of Action and Avoidance. Deconstructs the high-stakes productivity rhetoric—"skip one day and you wire your brain for avoidance," "only a karmic reckoning of willpower works"—against empirical neuroscience. Habit formation is asymptotic (median 66 days, Lally 2010), procrastination is an amygdala-driven emotional-regulation failure rather than a character flaw, and sleep is a mechanical prerequisite for prefrontal control. Synthesizes experience-dependent plasticity, Schlüter's 2018 amygdala/dACC imaging, Schultz's reward prediction error, Loewenstein's hot-cold empathy gap, and Inzlicht's process model of self-control.

📖 Why This Vault Exists

Knowledge picked up from a sentence is cheap and reversible—it stays in the head. This vault exists to convert earned knowledge into a durable, navigable structure: every topic is written out as an essay (the report) and rendered as an interactive page (the experience). Knowing needs evidence; a vault needs surrendering the chaos of unorganized notes into one indexed place.

Conventions: each subfolder is one topic. readme.md is the written report, index.html is the primary visualization, and numbered 0.html, 1.html… files are companion variants. This page links every HTML and Markdown file in every subfolder—when a new topic is earned, add a card above and a section to Readme.
