Paul Gilbert's CFT Framework

Intrapersonal Hostility & The Three Circle Model

When we experience failure or emotional distress, self-criticism mistakenly activates our evolutionary Threat & Self-Protection System. Using shame as a motivational tool fails because internal hostility triggers cortisol release and autonomic panic, effectively shutting down prefrontal problem-solving.

Adaptive regulation requires deliberate shift into the Soothing & Affiliative System through self-compassion and cognitive reframing. This parasympathetic state restores heart rate variability (HRV) and allows true psychological resilience.

Behavioral Activation & Emotion Regulation

The Two-Minute Rule & Breaking the Emotion Gap

Procrastination and lack of motivation are rarely time-management problems. They represent short-term affective mood repair: the brain prioritizes immediate relief from task-induced anxiety over long-term goals.

By implementing James Clear's and David Allen's Two-Minute Gateway Habit, we reduce task friction below the amygdala's threat-detection threshold. In Behavioral Activation (BA), action precedes motivation. Initiating an infinitesimally small step bypasses emotional avoidance and yields immediate neurobiological momentum.

"The intention-action gap is bridged not by sheer willpower, but by shrinking the initial step until emotional resistance drops to zero."

Relational Trauma & Attachment Theory

Hyperactivating Strategies vs. Direct Communication

When attachment threat is perceived, anxiously attached individuals engage in hyperactivating strategies—such as clinginess, chasing, and indirect protest behavior—to downregulate relational panic.

Similarly, people pleasing functions as the fawn response to secure safety through self-abandonment. Replacing indirect protest with direct asks for reassurance, and replacing appeasement with small boundaries, fulfills the true core need for relational safety without alienating partners.

Anxious: Hyperactivates (chases) to resolve panic -> Need: Reassurance
Avoidant: Deactivates (withdraws) to protect autonomy -> Need: Space
Secure: Communicates needs directly & manages co-regulation

Polyvagal Theory & Somatic State Shifts

Somatic Dysregulation: Snapping vs. Doom Scrolling

Our actions are downstream of our physiological state. Snapping is a hyperaroused sympathetic fight response triggered when environmental demands exceed our window of tolerance; implementing a physical pause allows prefrontal executive control to re-engage.

Conversely, Doom Scrolling often reflects a dorsal vagal freeze response—a state of passive immobilization where the brain consumes digital micro-dopamine hits to numb anxiety. Restoring regulation requires an active somatic state change (cold water, movement, grounding) rather than cognitive rationalization.

Jeffrey Young's Schema Therapy

The Schema Coping Spectrum & Healthy Adult Mode

Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS) trigger default coping styles when core childhood needs are unmet. These survival strategies map directly to biological threat modes: Surrender (Freeze/Fawn), Avoidance (Flight), and Overcompensation (Fight).

Categorization of Maladaptive Coping Styles

Figure 5.1: The distribution of analyzed coping behaviors across Schema Therapy coping styles.

🛡️ Surrender Mode (Freeze / Fawn)

Accepts schema as truth. Manifests as People Pleasing (Subjugation) and accepting guilt when canceling plans.

🏃 Avoidance Mode (Flight)

Escapes distress. Manifests as Procrastination, Doom Scrolling, and social withdrawal.

⚔️ Overcompensation Mode (Fight)

Fights internal threat excessively. Manifests as Snapping, Overthinking (control), and Clinginess (chasing).

🌱 The Ultimate Goal: Strengthening the Healthy Adult

The Healthy Adult mode recognizes the unmet need of the Vulnerable Child, silences the Punitive Critic with self-compassion, and replaces automatic threat responses with targeted adaptive actions.
