Section II: Cognitive Psychology & Behavioral Rules

The Cognitive Engine: Rule Governance & Insensitivity

Why do intelligent individuals remain locked in situations where every logical deduction is internally valid yet practically destructive? Here we dissect Relational Frame Theory (RFT), rule-based insensitivity to contingencies, Albert Ellis's "musturbation," and Gregory Bateson's double binds.

1

RFT: Three Rule Types

Steven Hayes' Relational Frame Theory outlines how verbal rules decouple behavior from direct feedback:

Pliance: Behavior driven by socially mediated compliance ("I must stay to avoid being seen as selfish"). Highly brittle; maintains traps via fear of social disapproval.
Tracking: Behavior guided by natural physical contingencies ("Follow this recipe to bake bread"). Fluid and adaptive to reality.
Augmenting: Rules that alter the emotional weight of consequences ("A true leader suffers in silence").
Result: Pliance causes chronic insensitivity to immediate pain.
2

The Trap of "Musturbation"

Albert Ellis (REBT) and Logic-Based Therapy pinpoint dogmatic premises that masquerade as pure logic:

Dogmatic Premise: "I must remain completely consistent with my past commitments, or I am worthless."
Scaffolding: "If I leave, I break a promise. Breaking promises is selfish. I am not allowed to be selfish."
The Impasse: Each logical step is sound, but the foundation is an unquestioned irrational demand.
Solution: Audit the primary axiom rather than debating deductions.
3

Bateson's Double Bind

Structural deadlock created by contradictory commands where comment is forbidden:

  • Primary injunction: "Perform task X or you fail."
  • Secondary conflicting injunction: "If you do X, you violate principle Y."
  • Tertiary meta-rule: "You are forbidden from calling attention to this paradox or withdrawing from the arena."
When trapped in a double bind, rational debate within the rules merely repeats the contradiction. The system requires an external rupture.
Escaping: Must break the tertiary meta-rule.
