Separating Valid Relational Psychology from Paranoid Bookkeeping

Internet relationship subcultures frequently distill human intimacy into three zero-sum mechanical seesaws: Value vs. Comfort, Leverage vs. Cooperation, and Sex vs. Purpose. While this model correctly observes that anxious neediness erodes attraction and personal autonomy matters, it wraps those truths in an adversarial framework of hostage negotiation and evolutionary storytelling—turning the practitioner into a hyper-vigilant emotional accountant.

✓ What Holds Up

Anxious over-pursuit spirals are self-defeating; collapsing your entire identity into a partner stifles erotic differentiation and mutual admiration.

✗ Where It Breaks

Conflating psychological security with complacency, mistaking coercion for cooperation, and relying on unfalsifiable "150,000-year-old" myths.

↺ The Core Paradox

Preaching unbothered detachment while demanding constant micro-monitoring of levers makes the practitioner more externally preoccupied than an over-texter.

Unconscious Systemic Polarization: Clinical psychologists like James Tobin use the "relational seesaw" to describe how one partner's rigid hyper-rationality unconsciously pushes the other partner into carrying the dyad's emotional ambivalence. It is a diagnostic observation of systemic imbalance, not a tactical weapon.

Empirical Mate Preferences vs. The "Hypergamy" Myth

David Buss's cross-cultural research (10,047 participants across 37 cultures) shows both sexes rank Kindness, Intelligence, and Mutual Attraction highest. While women value resource potential relatively more than men, reducing attraction to status/dread is a "Panglossian" just-so story.
